                                            mitsuhiko 2026-07-12 16:55:06

                                            You're throwing several very different things into one bucket.

                                            A bot opening 700 connections per second from one IP is badly written. I'm not encouraging that, defending it, or saying site operators should have to absorb denial-of-service-like traffic.

                                            What I am saying is that I'm generally supportive of scraping, and I think the default balance of power should favor the person accessing publicly available information and not the server operator claiming broad control over how that information may be read or processed.

                                            "Scraping should be permitted" is not the same position as "scrapers may consume unlimited resources and ignore basic network etiquette." You can oppose abusive traffic without turning every form of automated access into the idea of burning down the commons.

                                            Fundamentally from where I stand, just as Open Source should not discriminate based on who uses the software or what they use it for, access to public information should not depend on whether the publisher approves of the reader, the tool, or the purpose. The web is valuable precisely because information can be retrieved, indexed, transformed, compared, archived, and reused without requiring individual permission from every server operator.

                                            Information being freely accessible is part of the commons too. Turning the public web into a permissioned system where every automated reader needs the publisher's blessing would damage that commons far more deeply.
